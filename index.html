How to Use the Spain Income Tax Calculator

The Spain Income Tax Calculator computes IRPF (Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas) — Spanish income tax — plus employee social security contributions for 2025. It handles residents, applies the general tax scale, and includes the personal/family minimum deductions.

Enter your annual gross salary and autonomous community (region). Spain has both state and regional income tax components, with regions setting their own additional rates. The calculator shows the combined tax, social security (approximately 6.35% employee contribution), and net take-home pay.

A crucial nuance is the Beckham Law (Régimen Especial para Trabajadores Desplazados): qualifying foreign workers moving to Spain can opt to be taxed as non-residents for up to 6 years, paying a flat 24% rate on Spanish-sourced income up to €600,000 instead of the standard progressive rates. This is available via the Hacienda application and dramatically benefits high earners.

📊 Worked Example

€50,000 gross salary, resident in Madrid, 2025:

  • IRPF (state + Madrid regional): €9,870
  • Social Security (6.35%): €3,175
  • Total deductions: €13,045
  • Monthly take-home: €3,080
  • Under Beckham Law (flat 24%): €3,327/month

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Spanish income tax (IRPF) rates for 2025?

State IRPF rates (applied to all residents): 9.5% on the first €12,450; 12% on €12,451–€20,200; 15% on €20,201–€35,200; 18.5% on €35,201–€60,000; 22.5% on €60,001–€300,000; 24.5% above €300,000. Regional rates are additional on top, bringing total rates to approximately 19–47% depending on the autonomous community.

What is the Beckham Law in Spain?

The Beckham Law (Ley Beckham, article 93 IRPF) allows foreign workers who establish tax residency in Spain for work purposes to opt for the non-resident tax regime for up to 6 years. Instead of progressive rates up to 47%, they pay 24% on income up to €600,000 and 47% above. It's named after David Beckham who used it when joining Real Madrid.

How do Spanish social security contributions work?

Employees contribute approximately 6.35% of gross salary in social security: 4.7% for common contingencies (pension, maternity), 1.55% for unemployment, and 0.1% for vocational training. Employers pay an additional ~30%+ on top. The monthly maximum contribution base is ~€4,909 (2025), capping total contributions for high earners.

Do Spain's autonomous communities set their own taxes?

Yes. Each of Spain's 17 autonomous communities (regions) can set their own income tax rates for the regional component (approximately half of total IRPF). Madrid and Murcia have the lowest regional rates; Catalonia and Valencia have higher regional rates. For a €60,000 salary, tax can differ by €1,500–€3,000 between regions.

Is there a wealth tax in Spain?

Yes. Spain has an annual wealth tax (Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio) on net assets above €700,000 (€1,500,000 in some regions like Madrid which has a 100% bonus effectively eliminating it). Rates range from 0.2% to 3.5%. A temporary 'solidarity tax' (Impuesto de Solidaridad de Grandes Fortunas) was introduced for high-net-worth individuals above €3m.
